Short answer

A Special Police Officer (SPO) is a security officer who has been commissioned with arrest powers on a specific property. A regular guard can watch, document and call 911. An SPO can stop the person, detain them and hand them to police with a report already written.

What a Special Police Officer does

From the outside the job looks a lot like a security post: uniform, rounds, a desk or a gate. The difference shows up when something happens. If a shoplifter walks out with $800 of product, a guard watches and makes a phone call. An SPO can stop them at the door.

That changes how people behave on your property. Repeat offenders learn quickly which buildings have officers who can act, and they move on. Calls to police tend to drop, because problems get handled before they turn into calls. client to confirm

Detain and arrest

On the property named in the commission, under that jurisdiction's rules.

Write it up properly

Reports that police and prosecutors can use, written the same shift.

Hand off to police

A clean handover when MPD or county officers arrive.

When a site needs Special Police

Most of our SPO clients tried regular guards first. These are the usual signs it's time to upgrade:

  • The same people keep coming back to steal or trespass
  • Residents report loitering or drug activity in common areas
  • A public building has access rules that need enforcing
  • Parking garages see break-ins after dark
  • Retail is losing product to organized crews
  • Your insurer or landlord is asking for more

SPO vs. guard vs. off-duty police

Three options, three price points. A lot of sites end up with a mix: officers on the front desk, an SPO on the evening shift, and off-duty police for the holiday rush.

Special Police FAQ

Can Special Police Officers carry firearms?+

Yes, if the commission and your post orders allow it. Armed SPOs carry extra certification, and we'll explain what your site needs. client to confirm

Can a Special Police Officer arrest someone off the property?+

No. Their authority is tied to the property in the commission. Off the property, they have the same powers as any private citizen.

Are Special Police more expensive than security guards?+

Yes, somewhat. You're paying for the commission, extra training and insurance. For sites with recurring losses, it usually costs less than the losses it stops.

Do Special Police wear police uniforms?+

They wear a clearly marked GRDX Special Police uniform. It reads as official, but it isn't a city or county police uniform.

How long does it take to put an SPO on my site?+

It depends on the jurisdiction's commissioning process. We'll give you a realistic date after the site walk and can cover the gap with regular officers.

Need arrest authority?

Send the address and what's been happening. A supervisor will tell you whether an SPO is the right call.

Short answer

Church security usually starts with one or two licensed officers during services, placed at the main entrance and in the parking lot, working alongside the congregation's volunteer safety team. Larger churches add armed coverage, children's wing checks and a written emergency plan.

What a Sunday looks like with GRDX

Our officer arrives before the first service and walks the building with your safety lead. They check doors that should be locked, the children's area and the lot. During the service they stay visible at the main entrance, where most trouble starts, and keep a radio link to your team.

Officers are briefed on your congregation, and they greet people. Members should feel looked after. Someone looking for an easy target should notice the uniform and keep driving.

Armed or unarmed?

Most congregations start unarmed and add an armed officer after a threat assessment. Others go armed from day one because of a specific threat or something close to home. Both are reasonable. We'll give you our honest read on your building, your neighborhood and your history, and the decision stays with your leadership.

Working with your volunteer safety team

Plenty of churches already have members, often retired police or military, who keep an eye on things. We don't replace them. Our officers fit into the plan your team already runs, and we can train on de-escalation, medical response and radio procedure.
